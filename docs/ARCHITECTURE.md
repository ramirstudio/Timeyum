# Timeyum architecture

Timeyum reproduces the look of the ARRI Timing Shift Box: a film camera whose shutter is out of phase with the pull-down claw. The engine is a dependency-free C++17 library (`core/`) used by a SmartFX wrapper for After Effects (`ae/`) and by a command line tool (`tools/timeyum_cli.cpp`). It runs on the CPU only, on its own threads.

## Model

In a film camera the claw pulls the film down while the shutter is closed. With a timing shift the exposure happens while the film is still moving: every bright point draws a streak along the direction of travel, and the part of the streak that leaves the frame lands on the neighbouring frame, so it re-enters from the opposite edge. The engine computes this exposure as a circular convolution of the image with an oriented line kernel.

`Kernel.cpp` builds the kernel as a list of taps (offset in pixels along the direction, weight). The Camera profile simulates a 360 degree cycle: the film rests for (360 - pulldown) degrees and then advances one frame pitch during `pulldown_angle` degrees, with an adjustable acceleration (`claw_ease`). The shutter is open for `shutter_angle` degrees starting at `timing_shift`. The distribution of film positions while the shutter is open gives both the share of light that stays sharp and the shape of the streak. The other profiles (Fade, Exponential, Curve) are artistic: a weight as a function of the position along the streak, normalised to the `smear` share. Ghosts are extra taps at fixed distances. The sum of the weights is the share of the exposure that leaves the base image, so in Exposure mode the average brightness is conserved.

## Render

`Timeyum.cpp` converts the frame to linear float (`ColorSpace.cpp`: sRGB, gamma 2.4, LogC3, S-Log3), isolates the highlights with a threshold and a soft knee, and convolves the planes with the kernel through a 2D FFT. The FFT domain depends on the edge mode. With Wrap it is exactly the frame (plus the `frame_gap` rows); with Extend, Mirror and Black it is the frame plus a margin equal to the streak, rounded up to a size that factors into 2, 3 and 5. Two real planes that share a kernel travel in a single complex FFT. Lateral chromatic spread uses a kernel of different length per channel. After the convolution come gain, breakup (intensity variation between streaks), saturation and tint of the smear only, the blend (Exposure, Add, Screen, Lighten), the vertical frame roll with its frame line, the gate weave and the final opacity.

The shake (`Noise.cpp`) is deterministic value noise of time and seed, so the same frame always renders identically. `Fft.cpp` is a Stockham FFT with radix 2, 3, 4, 5 and a generic radix up to 7, and Bluestein for sizes with larger prime factors.

## Warp

Warp makes the streaks non-uniform and lets them react to the video. A scalar field and a vector field are computed on a low resolution grid (about 192 nodes along the long side, `Warp.cpp`) and interpolated bilinearly, so the result is continuous in space.

The field has three ingredients. The flow is multi-octave 3D value noise with time as the third coordinate, so it evolves continuously and without state: the same frame renders identically every time, even out of order. From the noise come a streak length multiplier (the `tanh` of a lower, wider octave stack) and a displacement, which is the curl of a scalar potential: it is divergence-free, so it moves the image like a fluid without compressing it. The high octaves of the potential carry less weight because the displacement uses the gradient, which amplifies high frequencies. The reaction to the video starts from the brightness on the low resolution grid, compressed by a soft curve and blurred, and from motion, the weighted mean of the frame to frame differences over the recent past. The weights decay with the inertia, and the wrapper asks After Effects for up to 8 previous frames (checkouts at earlier times), so there is no state between renders here either. The interactive point and the automatic target, the centroid of what is brighter than the frame average, produce a Gaussian push: a bulge or pinch of the displacement and a local lengthening of the streaks.

The length varies per pixel through a scatter model. A few length levels are computed (6 by default, from `buildLevels`, which always contains level 1). Each source pixel distributes its light between the two neighbouring levels with tent weights, each level is convolved with its own kernel, and the convolutions are summed in the frequency domain, so one inverse FFT per plane is enough. The exposure removed from the sharp image follows the same weights, so in Exposure mode with Wrap the energy is conserved exactly. Levels that no pixel uses in a frame are skipped without changing the result. With every modulation at zero there is a single level and the result is identical to the effect without warp.

The displacement is applied after the convolution: the smear is sampled with the full displacement, the sharp image only with the `base_follow` share. The wave part is flattened, by a single factor per frame, when the Jacobian of the mapping would drop below 0.15, because a folded mapping produces ragged edges. The bulge and pinch never fold up to 100%. The control views (length map, reaction, displacement) come from the same field.

## After Effects wrapper

`TimeyumAE.cpp` implements SmartFX (`PreRender`, `SmartRender`) at 8, 16 and 32 bits per channel, with the multi-frame rendering flags. The streak wraps around the whole layer, so every render requests the whole layer. Parameters have indices in panel order (`TimeyumParams.h`) and stable on-disk IDs that are only ever appended. After Effects colour is premultiplied: for non-linear spaces the wrapper unpremultiplies, decodes, premultiplies again and runs the engine in linear light, then takes the reverse path. The resolution factor (half, third) converts parameters expressed in pixels.

At the top of the panel sits a parameter without data (`P_BANNER`, flag `PF_PUI_CONTROL`) drawn with Drawbot by the `PF_Cmd_EVENT` handler. The picture is a raw BGRA block (`TimeyumBanner.bin`, generated by `tools/make_banner.py`) embedded as the `TY_BANNER_IMAGE` resource, fitted to the width of the row with a centred crop. The scheme was worked out first in Lensyum: the custom UI must be registered with an all-zero `register_ui`, `PF_OutFlag_CUSTOM_UI` goes both in `GlobalSetup` and in the PiPL, the row is drawn in two events (title and control) so the picture is shared over the union of the two, pixels go to Drawbot as straight BGRA, and the resource name must not equal the `TIMEYUM_BANNER` macro. It can be switched off with `-DTIMEYUM_BANNER=OFF`. The flags of `GlobalSetup` and of the PiPL come from `TimeyumFlags.h`, with `static_assert`s against the SDK flags. The PiPL is generated with PiPLtool at build time, or a pre-generated `.rc` can be passed with `TIMEYUM_PIPL_RC`.

## Verification

`tests/test_fft.cpp` compares the FFT with a direct DFT at 28 sizes. `tests/compare_reference.py` compares the command line tool with the NumPy reference engine (`tests/reference/`) on 23 configurations and two resolutions, one with sizes that go through Bluestein: the largest relative error is 7e-5, typically 1e-7. `tests/test_warp.cpp` checks the properties of the warp: the levels form a partition of unity, with amount zero or nothing to modulate the result equals the plain effect, length modulation alone conserves the average brightness, the result does not depend on the thread count, the field is continuous in time (one frame against one second) and in space, the displacement mapping does not fold, the reaction follows motion and has memory, the point pushes in the right direction and fades away, and the automatic target finds the bright spot. `tests/test_ae_wrapper.cpp` compiles the wrapper against a mock SDK (`tests/ae_mock/`) and checks parameter order and IDs, that the panel defaults equal the engine defaults, the enabling logic, pixel conversion at 8, 16 and 32 bits, the past-frame checkouts in pre-render and render, a complete warp render with history against the engine, and the banner parameter (position, ID, flags, UI registration). The wrapper compiles against After Effects SDK 26.5. The Drawbot drawing of the banner and the behaviour of the point control are not covered by the automated tests and need a manual check in After Effects.

## Limits

The `.aex` builds on Windows with MSVC only. The panel has no drawable curve (the Curve profile exists in the engine and in the command line tool). Without Warp the kernel is the same over the whole frame. At 4K a frame takes about 2 s on 4 threads, and Warp costs 1.4 to 1.8 times the plain effect; the length steps ("Length Steps") are the main lever. The reaction to motion looks at the past but not the future, and there is no state between frames, so with high inertia the first frame of a clip has no history to start from. The interactive point is read in pixels at the current preview resolution.
